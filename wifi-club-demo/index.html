A members' club for working guest Wi-Fi

Working prototype · synthetic data only

A small membership - about HK$10 a month, proposed, nothing charged - for convenience and reliability: find an authorised guest network, connect with less hassle, and know it worked recently. The venue's free Wi-Fi stays the venue's; members pay for the club, never for the Wi-Fi itself.

How it works

  1. 1Find a venue nearby in the app. Anyone can see scores and network names.
  2. 2Members tap Connect - the app asks iOS to join the saved network. iOS may ask you to approve first, and the phone itself does the connecting.
  3. 3The app never joins networks for you and never sees your other Wi-Fi passwords.
  4. 4Back in the app, tap Worked or Didn't work. That single tap is the freshness engine.
  5. 5When failures pile up, the entry drops in score and goes back for review or an update from someone on the spot.

The subscription, roughly

Proposed price
About HK$10 per month - a hypothesis to test, not a set price. App Store prices come in fixed tiers, so the real price would be the nearest HK tier.
Store economics
A digital subscription in an iPhone app generally must use Apple in-app purchase (App Store guideline 3.1.1, with regional exceptions elsewhere). Apple keeps roughly 15-30%, leaving about HK$7-8.50 per member at HK$10.
Cost to start
Publishing any iPhone app needs an Apple Developer Program membership - about US$99 a year on Apple's published terms. Check the current fee before committing.
Free tier
Scores, network names and open networks stay free, so non-paying users still feed reports and contributions.
Reference point
WiFi Map's premium was about US$35 a year on its US App Store listing (HK pricing not checked). This club would compete on verified freshness in one city, not database size.

Tap-to-connect on a real iPhone

Finding the network
iOS gives apps no way to scan nearby Wi-Fi, so the venue comes from the app's own database by location - you tap the venue in the app, not the system Wi-Fi list.
Joining
The app can ask iOS to join with the saved password (NEHotspotConfiguration). Apple documents that the device prompts you to approve adding the network, approval is not connection - the phone joins only if the network is found nearby, and even a successful join doesn't guarantee working internet.
Sign-in pages
Networks with a captive portal (many lounges and hotels) still show their sign-in or terms page. Automatic portal login needs Apple's Hotspot Helper entitlement, granted case by case.
Checking it worked
An app can read the joined network's name when it configured the join itself (or with precise location permission, VPN, or DNS configurations), then probe real internet. It cannot silently test a password in advance - your Worked tap stays the core signal.

What a real version needs

What members pay for
Convenience, reliability and freshness, plus authorised connectivity. No ownership or resale claim over a venue's free Wi-Fi.
Sourcing
Guest networks only, shared by visitors or confirmed by the venue itself. Private, home and staff networks are refused at submission.
Access conditions
Knowing a password is not permission to use a network. Entries carry conditions like "customers only"; members-only lounge networks stay out unless the operator opts in.
Moderation
Apple requires filtering, reporting and blocking for user-contributed content (App Store guideline 1.2), so review sits in front of publication.
Security
Credentials stored encrypted, revealed only to members, and venues can claim their listing to rotate or retire details.
The honest limitA failed report can mean a changed password, a captive portal step, or a weak signal - the score treats them together and freshness dates keep that visible.
